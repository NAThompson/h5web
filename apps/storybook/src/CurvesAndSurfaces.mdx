# Curves and surfaces with a scalar field in HDF5

_Convention used by the H5Web app (`@h5web/app`) to show a dataset as a **3D curve** or a **parametric surface** coloured by a scalar field (the "Curve" and "Surface" visualizations). Version 5: view hints as typed `H5WEB_\*` attributes (v1, phase 1), §5.\_

The idea, borrowed from ParaView: a curve or surface is a set of points in 3D, and a scalar field is defined on those points (time, curvature, temperature...). The convention reuses existing standards rather than inventing attributes:

- the **CF conventions' `coordinates` attribute** ([CF §5, auxiliary coordinate variables](https://cfconventions.org/Data/cf-conventions/cf-conventions-1.11/cf-conventions.html#coordinate-system)) to attach point coordinates to a field — exactly how NetCDF files describe curvilinear grids and trajectories;
- **HDF5 dimension scales** ([HDF5 Dimension Scales](https://support.hdfgroup.org/documentation/hdf5/latest/_h5_d_s__u_g.html)) as an alternative way to attach the coordinates of a curve;
- the **NeXus `@interpretation="vertex"`** value ([NXdata](https://manual.nexusformat.org/classes/base_classes/NXdata.html)) for an array of points without a field;
- the **CF `featureType`** attribute ([CF §9, discrete sampling geometries](https://cfconventions.org/Data/cf-conventions/cf-conventions-1.11/cf-conventions.html#discrete-sampling-geometries)) to tell a trajectory (connected points) from other point sets;
- CF `axis`, `standard_name`, `units`, `positive` and `long_name` attributes to identify X, Y, Z and label the axes.

String attributes may be scalar strings or arrays holding a single string (as some writers store them); attributes of any other type are treated as absent.

## 1. Field on a curve or surface: CF `coordinates`

A numeric dataset `F` is a scalar field on a curve or a surface if it has a string attribute `coordinates` listing (blank-separated) the names of the datasets holding the X, Y and Z coordinates of its points. Fewer than three names is never a geometry (and costs no further request).

- **Name resolution** ([CF 1.8 groups](https://cfconventions.org/Data/cf-conventions/cf-conventions-1.11/cf-conventions.html#groups)):
  - absolute path (`/grid/x`): as is;
  - relative path (`../x`, `./sub/x`, `sub/x`): relative to the group containing `F`, `..` never going above the root;
  - bare name (`x`): searched by proximity - the group containing `F`, then each ancestor group up to the root; the nearest match wins.
- **Matching coordinates**: a listed dataset is a coordinate of the geometry if it is numeric, 1D or 2D, and its shape equals the **trailing dimensions** of `F`. Other listed variables (scalar coordinates such as a time stamp, string labels, variables that do not exist) are ignored, as CF allows `coordinates` to list them.
- **Which is X, Y, Z** - the role of each matching coordinate is identified as in CF §4, by order of precedence:
  1. `axis` attribute: `X`, `Y`, `Z` or `T`;
  2. `standard_name`: `longitude`, `grid_longitude`, `projection_x_coordinate` → X; `latitude`, `grid_latitude`, `projection_y_coordinate` → Y; `height`, `altitude`, `depth`, `height_above_*`, `air_pressure` → Z; `time` → T;
  3. `units`: `degrees_east` (and variants `degree_E`, `degreesE`...) → X; `degrees_north` (and variants) → Y; a time reference such as `"s since 2000-01-01"` or `"days since 1970-01-01"` → T;
  4. `positive` = `up` or `down` → Z.

  Time coordinates are then left out. Each of X, Y, Z must be held by exactly one coordinate - with one allowance: if a single role is missing and a single coordinate is unidentified, it takes that role (e.g. longitude and latitude identified by their units, plus an altitude in metres). Only when **no** coordinate is identified are the remaining coordinates taken as X, Y, Z in the order listed, and exactly three must remain. So with `lat` and `lon` identified, X is longitude and Y latitude, whatever their order in `coordinates`.

- **Kind of geometry** - given by the rank of the coordinates (all three must have the same shape):
  - 1D coordinates of shape `(N,)`: **curve**, a polyline through the N points in index order - _only for trajectories_: 1D auxiliary coordinates do not imply that points are connected (CF `timeSeries` or `point` features, station networks...). The points form a curve only if `F` has a `featureType` attribute equal to `trajectory` (opt-in for any file), or else if the file's global (root) `featureType` attribute is `trajectory`. A `featureType` on `F` overrides the global one;
  - 2D coordinates of shape `(Nu, Nv)`: **parametric surface** x(u, v), y(u, v), z(u, v), triangulated along the grid (at least 2 × 2) - grid connectivity is implied, so no `featureType` is needed.
- **Extra dimensions**: `F` may have leading dimensions in front of the geometry dimensions, e.g. `(Nt, Nu, Nv)` for a field that evolves over time on a fixed surface. They are sliced with the dimension mapper.
- **Missing values**: `NaN` (or a CF `_FillValue`, `valid_min`/`valid_max`/`valid_range` value) in coordinates or in the field leaves a gap in a curve and a hole in a surface. Packed NetCDF data (`scale_factor`, `add_offset`) is unpacked.
- **Labels**: axis labels are the coordinates' `long_name` (or dataset name), followed by their `units` in parentheses.

Exactly two coordinates is _not_ a geometry: `temperature(y, x)` with `coordinates = "lon lat"` is the usual NetCDF curvilinear grid, and stays a heatmap.

See the [complete example](#examples-h5py) at the end.

## 2. Field on a curve: HDF5 dimension scales

Instead of a `coordinates` attribute, the X, Y (and optionally Z) coordinates of a curve can be attached to the **last dimension** of the field `F` as HDF5 dimension scales (h5py: `make_scale` then `dims[-1].attach_scale`). `F` may have leading dimensions, which are sliced.

- **Roles**: identified as in rule 1 (`axis`, `standard_name`, `units`, `positive`), and finally by the scale's `NAME` (the name given to `make_scale`) if it is literally `x`, `y` or `z`. X and Y are required, Z is optional (a planar curve without Z lies in the `z = 0` plane). Each role must be held by a single scale.
- **Alternatives**: HDF5 allows several scales on one dimension, usually as alternative axes (e.g. time in seconds and in hours, or bin centres and edges). Scales that do not identify as X, Y or Z, time scales, and scales whose length differs from the dimension's are ignored - in particular, a dimension carrying only alternative scales is never a curve.
- **Connectivity**: as for 1D `coordinates`, the points form a curve only for trajectories (`featureType = "trajectory"` on `F`, or else on the root). A station time series with latitude, longitude and altitude scales stays a heatmap or line plot.
- **Precedence**: when `F` has a `coordinates` attribute, only rule 1 applies.
- **Surfaces**: dimension scales are 1D by definition (one value per index of a single dimension), so they cannot give the 2D coordinate grids of a parametric surface: surfaces need `coordinates`.

Providers that cannot dereference `DIMENSION_LIST` (e.g. some remote servers) do not support this form.

```python
knot_ds = f.create_group("knot_dimscales")
knot_ds["t"] = t
knot_ds["t"].attrs["featureType"] = "trajectory"
for name, data in zip("xyz", (x, y, z)):
    knot_ds[name] = data
    knot_ds[name].make_scale(name)  # NAME "x", "y" or "z" identifies the role
    knot_ds["t"].dims[0].attach_scale(knot_ds[name])
```

## 3. Curve without a field: NeXus `interpretation="vertex"`

A numeric dataset of shape `(..., N, 2)` or `(..., N, 3)` with attribute `interpretation = "vertex"` is a **curve** through its N rows (x, y[, z]), coloured by point index (i.e. by its parameter). A 2-column array lies in the `z = 0` plane. Leading dimensions, if any, are sliced. The dataset's `units` attribute labels all three axes.

An opt-in attribute is required on purpose: `(N, 3)` arrays are common (tables, RGB colours, Cartesian vectors) and must not be hijacked. `vertex` is the NeXus `NXdata/@interpretation` value meaning "the last dimension holds the coordinates of a vertex", so it is reused rather than inventing a new term.

```python
pts = f.create_dataset("helix", data=np.stack([np.cos(t), np.sin(t), t / 5], axis=-1))
pts.attrs["interpretation"] = "vertex"
```

When a dataset has a `coordinates` attribute or a dimension-scale curve, those rules apply first.

## 4. NeXus (recommended)

**Recommended layout**: put each field and its coordinates in a NeXus `NXdata` group whose `signal` attribute names the field, and point the file's (or `NXentry`'s) `default` attribute to the group to open first. Selecting the group in H5Web, or the file itself, then opens the Curve or Surface visualization directly, without having to find the field among its coordinates.

A NeXus `NXdata` group whose `@signal` dataset follows rule 1, 2 or 3 is offered as "NX Curve" / "NX Surface" (selected by default). NeXus has no way to give a 2D signal three spatial axes (`@axes` has one entry per signal dimension), so the signal carries the CF `coordinates` attribute - NeXus allows extra attributes. For NeXus-aware tools, also declare which signal dimensions each coordinate depends on with `AXISNAME_indices`:

```python
nxdata = entry.create_group("torus")
nxdata.attrs.update({"NX_class": "NXdata", "signal": "K", "axes": [".", "."]})
for name in "xyz":
    nxdata[name] = f[f"torus/{name}"]
    nxdata.attrs[f"{name}_indices"] = [0, 1]
nxdata["K"] = f["torus/K"]  # has `coordinates = "x y z"`
```

## 5. View hints: `H5WEB_*` attributes

The space of visualizations is infinite, and a viewer with an option for everything becomes unusable. Instead, the **file declares how it is meant to be viewed**, with plain typed attributes on the objects they describe: view settings on the dataset (or `NXdata` group), overlay geometry and style on the overlay object. H5Web opens the declared visualization with the declared settings, so the toolbar can stay small. Files without hints are shown as before (heuristics). Attributes marked _Phase 2/3_ in the v1 schema are reserved (ignored for now).

- **Types**: strings (case-insensitive values; fixed- or variable-length), numbers, 1D float arrays; a scalar may be stored as a 1-element array (netCDF-4). Booleans are integers of any width (nonzero = true) or h5py booleans. Paths to other objects are space-separated strings, as CF `coordinates`. Everything works in netCDF-4 (no references, no compounds).
- **Forgiving**: unknown `H5WEB_*` attributes are ignored; an attribute of the wrong type or value is ignored, with a small note over the visualization; attributes that can't be read drop the hint (the entity opens as without hint). Nothing is logged to the console.
- **Suggestions, not locks**: hinted settings apply when the dataset is opened; any change by the user wins while the dataset stays open (like NeXus `SILX_style`). The hinted visualization is selected each time the dataset is opened (like NeXus `interpretation`).
- **Precedence**: `H5WEB_*` > NeXus (`default`, `interpretation`, `SILX_style`) > heuristics. On an `NXdata` group, the signal's attributes apply, overridden attribute by attribute by the group's; overlay paths are relative to the object that lists them.

### Overlays

`H5WEB_overlays` lists objects to draw on top of the dataset (the **base**), in its **data coordinates** - values of its axes (dimension scales or NeXus axes), not indices - on the heatmap and on the 3D surface (other visualizations note that overlays are not drawn). Each listed object says what it draws with `H5WEB_geometry` and carries its own style, written once and shared by every dataset that lists it:

| `H5WEB_geometry` | Object                                                                                                                                                                   | Draws                                                                                                      |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `points`         | `(N, d)` dataset (or `(d,)` for one point)                                                                                                                               | markers                                                                                                    |
| `curve`          | `(N, d)` dataset                                                                                                                                                         | polyline; a row with a NaN breaks it                                                                       |
| `ellipse`        | group with `H5WEB_mean` (path, `(d,)`), `H5WEB_cov` (path, `(d, d)`), optional `H5WEB_sigma` (path of a scalar dataset σ) and `H5WEB_nsigma` (float[], default `[1, 2]`) | covariance ellipses of N(mean, σ² cov) at `nsigma` standard deviations - marginal on the two shown columns |
| `text`           | scalar string dataset (the text) with `H5WEB_at` (float[2]) and `H5WEB_anchor` (`top`, `bottom`, `left`, `right`, `center`: side of `at` where the text goes)            | text annotation                                                                                            |

- **Paths**: absolute (`/coalescence/edge`), relative to the base's group (`../run/mean`, `sub/x`), or a bare name also searched in ancestor groups (CF-style, §1). For a hint on an `NXdata` group, relative to that group. Paths written on an overlay object (`H5WEB_color_by`, `H5WEB_z`, `H5WEB_mean`...) are relative to the group holding that object (bare names are searched from there up), so one overlay can be shared by bases anywhere.
- **`H5WEB_columns`**: `"alpha theta"` names the base dimension (by dimension-scale name) held by each column, and by each component of `H5WEB_at`; columns naming no base dimension are not drawn. Default: column 0 = last HDF5 dimension (x), column 1 = second-to-last (y). Writers should always give it. Overlays follow the axes when the user swaps them.
- **Coordinates are sample positions**: a coordinate equal to an axis value is where that sample is drawn - at the centre of its pixel on heatmaps (which draw each of N axis values as the start of a pixel; with N + 1 bin edges, coordinates are used as is), at the vertex on 3D surfaces.
- **Style** (all optional): `H5WEB_color` (CSS colour: lines, points without `H5WEB_color_by`, text), `H5WEB_size` (point radius, px), `H5WEB_width` (line width, px), `H5WEB_dash` (int), `H5WEB_halo` (int, default 1: contrasting halo around lines, points and text), `H5WEB_stroke` (outline of points), `H5WEB_label`, `H5WEB_label_mode` (`legend` default, or `inline`: drawn next to the overlay), `H5WEB_color_by` (path to an `(N,)` dataset colouring the points with the base colour map), `H5WEB_z` (3D: path to an `(N,)` dataset of heights, `value` = the `color_by` values, or `surface` - default; without `H5WEB_columns`, the third column of `(N, 3)` points is their height), `H5WEB_trail` (see linking).
- **Robustness**: overlays are drawn above the image - NaN pixels never hide them - and are not clipped at the edges of the domain. An overlay that can't be resolved (missing object, no geometry, wrong shape, unreadable values) is skipped with a small note; it never breaks the base view. Values larger than the automatic fetch limit (500 MB) are skipped with a note; point sets of more than 5000 points are drawn as plain GL points.
- **3D**: text overlays, dashes and inline labels are not drawn on 3D surfaces (a note says so).
- **Style is per overlay object**: to draw the same data twice in different styles (e.g. a mean as a trail and as an ellipse centre), the ellipse references the data by path and only the drawn object carries style.

### Linking: shared dimension scales

Datasets are **linked** - sliced and played together - when the **same HDF5 dimension scale** is attached to one of their dimensions (e.g. `generation` on the first dimension of the population, fitness, mean, covariance and step size of an optimizer; h5py: `make_scale`, `dims[0].attach_scale`):

- if the base has a dimension with that scale (e.g. a landscape per generation `(G, ny, nx)`), its slider slices the overlays too, and playback prefetches their slices with the base's;
- if it doesn't (e.g. a fixed landscape `f(x, y)`), H5Web adds a **linked slider** named after the scale below the dimension mapper. `H5WEB_slice_by = "<scale name or path>"` on the base names the scale; on an overlay object, it names the scale linking the overlay's leading dimension, attached or not (e.g. netCDF-4 data whose dimensions are phony). Else the scale on the first dimension of an overlay dataset that needs slicing is used.
- scales of the base's axes (its last two dimensions) are never taken as links unless named by `H5WEB_slice_by`: a curve along the x axis with the x scale attached is drawn whole.
- netCDF-4 phony dimension scales (`NAME` "This is a netCDF dimension but not a netCDF variable") are never axes, links, nor column names.
- `H5WEB_trail = 1` (curves): rows up to the current index only, e.g. the path of the mean so far.

Axis settings on dimension scales (`H5WEB_axis_scale`, `H5WEB_ticks`...) are _Phase 2_ and not read yet.

Overlay datasets with no linked dimension are drawn whole on every slice (static overlays). A dataset with no dimension scale at all whose first dimension has the linked length is linked by that dimension (for providers that cannot read dimension scales).

### Examples (h5py)

```python

c = f.create_group("coalescence")
for name, data, geometry, label in [("electron_nucleus_1", [[0, 0], [0, np.pi]], "curve", "r_{1} = 0"),
                                    ("electron_electron", [[np.pi / 2, 0]], "points", "r_{12} = 0")]:
    c[name] = data
    c[name].attrs.update({"H5WEB_geometry": geometry, "H5WEB_columns": "alpha theta",
                          "H5WEB_color": "#000", "H5WEB_label": label, "H5WEB_label_mode": "inline"})
f["psi30"].attrs.update({"long_name": "ψ_{3,0}(α, θ)", "H5WEB_vis": "heatmap", "H5WEB_scale": "symlog",
                         "H5WEB_colormap": "auto", "H5WEB_keep_ratio": 1,
                         "H5WEB_overlays": "/coalescence/electron_nucleus_1 /coalescence/electron_electron"})

# Optimizer run on a landscape, linked by the `generation` scale
gen = run.create_dataset("generation", data=np.arange(G))
gen.make_scale("generation")
for name in ("population", "fitness", "mean", "C", "sigma"):  # (G, λ, 2), (G, λ), (G, 2), (G, 2, 2), (G,)

# Convergence curves: (G, n) with generation on dim 0, all n lines, log y
```

## Not covered (yet)

- Coordinates that vary along the sliced dimensions (moving geometry): coordinates must match the trailing dimensions only.
- Unstructured meshes (triangles with explicit connectivity), closed-curve flags, vector fields.
- Planar curves via `coordinates` need an explicit Z coordinate (dimension scales may omit it) (or use a vertex array for 2-column data). A flat coordinate (e.g. Z = 0 everywhere) gives a box of zero thickness along that axis.
- `trajectoryProfile` and other CF discrete sampling geometries: only `featureType = "trajectory"` makes 1D coordinates a curve. Ragged arrays (contiguous or indexed) are not supported.

## Library components

The app renders these datasets with two components of `@h5web/lib`, which can be used directly:

- [`Curve3DVis`](?path=/docs/visualizations-curve3dvis--docs) - props `x`, `y`, `z?` (`NumArray`) and `values?` (scalar field, defaults to point indices), plus the usual `domain`, `colorMap`, `scaleType`, `invertColorMap`, `curveType`, `lineWidth`, `pointSize`, `keepRatio`, `showGrid`...
- [`ParamSurfaceVis`](?path=/docs/visualizations-paramsurfacevis--docs) - props `x`, `y`, `z` and `values` (2D `NdArray`s of the same shape), plus `representation` (`SurfaceRepresentation.Surface`, `Wireframe` or `SurfaceWithEdges`), `shading`, `maxVertices`...

## Examples (h5py)

Every group of this file is an `NXdata` group, so selecting it opens its visualization; the file opens the knot.

```python
"""Examples of the H5Web curves and surfaces convention (see CONVENTION.md).

Each group is a NeXus NXdata group whose `signal` is the field, so selecting the group in
H5Web opens the Curve or Surface visualization directly; the root `default` opens the knot.
"""

import h5py
import numpy as np


def nxdata(f, name, signal):
    group = f.create_group(name)
    group.attrs["NX_class"] = "NXdata"
    group.attrs["signal"] = signal
    return group


with h5py.File("conv_example.h5", "w") as f:
    f.attrs["NX_class"] = "NXroot"
    f.attrs["default"] = "knot"  # opened when the file is selected

    # Curve: Lissajous knot coloured by its parameter t (CF `coordinates`)
    t = np.linspace(0, 2 * np.pi, 1000)
    x, y, z = np.cos(3 * t + 0.7), np.cos(5 * t + 1), np.cos(7 * t)
    knot = nxdata(f, "knot", signal="t")
    knot["x"], knot["y"], knot["z"] = x, y, z
    knot["t"] = t
    knot["t"].attrs["coordinates"] = "x y z"
    knot["t"].attrs["featureType"] = "trajectory"  # points are connected

    # Same curve, with x, y and z attached to t as HDF5 dimension scales instead
    knot_ds = nxdata(f, "knot_dimscales", signal="t")
    knot_ds["t"] = t
    knot_ds["t"].attrs["featureType"] = "trajectory"
    for name, data in zip("xyz", (x, y, z)):
        knot_ds[name] = data
        knot_ds[name].make_scale(name)  # NAME "x", "y" or "z" identifies the role
        knot_ds["t"].dims[0].attach_scale(knot_ds[name])

    # Parametric surface: torus coloured by its Gaussian curvature
    u, v = np.meshgrid(np.linspace(0, 2 * np.pi, 64), np.linspace(0, 2 * np.pi, 128), indexing="ij")
    R, r = 2.0, 0.7
    torus = nxdata(f, "torus", signal="K")
    torus["x"] = (R + r * np.cos(u)) * np.cos(v)
    torus["y"] = (R + r * np.cos(u)) * np.sin(v)
    torus["z"] = r * np.sin(u)
    for name in "xyz":
        torus[name].attrs["units"] = "m"
    K = torus.create_dataset("K", data=np.cos(u) / (r * (R + r * np.cos(u))))
    K.attrs["coordinates"] = "x y z"
    K.attrs["long_name"] = "Gaussian curvature"
    K.attrs["units"] = "1/m^2"

    # Time-dependent field on the same surface (leading dimension is sliced)
    wave = torus.create_dataset("wave", data=np.stack([np.sin(3 * v - p) for p in np.linspace(0, 6, 10)]))
    wave.attrs["coordinates"] = "/torus/x /torus/y /torus/z"  # absolute paths work too

    # CF trajectory: roles identified by `axis`, `standard_name` and `units`; `time` is left out
    traj = nxdata(f, "trajectory", signal="temp")
    s = np.linspace(0, 20, 500)
    for name, data, attrs in [
        ("time", s, {"units": "s since 2026-01-01"}),
        ("alt", s / 4, {"standard_name": "altitude", "units": "km"}),
        ("lat", np.sin(s), {"units": "degrees_north"}),
        ("lon", np.cos(s), {"axis": "X", "units": "degrees_east"}),
    ]:
        traj[name] = data
        traj[name].attrs.update(attrs)
    traj["temp"] = 15 - s
    traj["temp"].attrs["coordinates"] = "time alt lat lon"
    traj["temp"].attrs["featureType"] = "trajectory"  # or as a global attribute: f.attrs["featureType"]

    # Station time series: same kind of coordinates, but not a curve (stations are not connected):
    # shown as an ordinary heatmap (time x station) or lines
    stations = nxdata(f, "stations", signal="temp")
    rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
    alt = rng.uniform(0, 2000, size=20)  # m
    for name, data, units in [
        ("lat", rng.uniform(44, 48, size=20), "degrees_north"),
        ("lon", rng.uniform(4, 10, size=20), "degrees_east"),
        ("alt", alt, "m"),
    ]:
        stations[name] = data
        stations[name].attrs["units"] = units
    hours = np.arange(96)  # 4 days, hourly
    diurnal = 6 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * (hours - 9) / 24)  # warmest mid-afternoon
    lapse = -6.5e-3 * alt  # colder higher up
    temp = 12 + diurnal[:, None] + lapse[None, :] + rng.normal(0, 0.8, size=(96, 20))
    stations["temp"] = temp  # (time, station)
    stations["temp"].attrs["coordinates"] = "lat lon alt"
    stations["temp"].attrs["featureType"] = "timeSeries"
    stations["temp"].attrs["units"] = "degC"
    stations["time"] = hours
    stations["time"].attrs["units"] = "h"
    stations.attrs["axes"] = ["time", "."]  # NeXus axes: time along the first dimension
```
